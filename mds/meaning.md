# Meaning

There's one thing in particular I've been grieving throughout the past few months before I die. Something that I feel, in an abstract sort of way, will be lost to the universe with my departure, and that has value. And I think that is **the ability to find meaning**; or, more specifically, the meanings that may never be found in quite the way I would, had I the chance to.

Maybe that's one of the final purposes of all my aspirations and interests and endeavors: to not only **create** meaning, but to **find** it. The _almost sacred_ act of creating something inherently imbues that something with meaning. But meaning can also be found, discovered, and given; bestowed.

I've always been one to say that everything happens for a reason - and not in the philosophical sense, but the logical. Cause and effect. Things happen because a number of factors combined and aligned _just so_. From why matter in the universe organizes itself into clusters, galaxies and starsystems, to why you ate what you ate on your last meal. What factors contributed to something may be unknown, even unknowable, but they exist. And though not everything has a meaning, everything has a reason - and, often, to find the meaning, or a deeper meaning, one must find the reason. And one of the main tools with one finds reason is my ever-so-present, ever-so-important, **logic**.

And though every living person has the capacity - even a natural, biological instinct - to find and give meaning to things, everyone does so differently. And for all my flaws and inabilities, and my dissonant lack of self-esteem, I **value** the way I see the world. I... **love** the way I see the world. I love my curiosity, and all the knowledge and experience it brought me in life, and the ability that all gives me to see things in ways so many never could. Ways that I've yearned to, and often eagerly did, share, despite great hesitance at its reception. Ways that I... still could've shared.

But that all dies with me.
The world has lost my eyes,
and with them all they could see.
